# Asset Integrity and Non-Destructive Inspection Guidelines (AIG-NDT-002)

## 1. Ultrasonic Thickness Gauging (UTG) Protocols

### 1.1 Measurement Points and Grid Layout
Piping circuits subject to high turbulence, flow direction changes (elbows, tees, reducers), and phase changes must have designated Critical Thickness Inspection Locations (CML/TML). Ultrasonic thickness measurements must be taken in a 4-point quadrant grid around the circumference at 1.5 pipe diameters upstream and downstream of all weld seams and fittings.

### 1.2 Minimum Required Wall Thickness (t_min) Calculation
The minimum allowable wall thickness {min}$ for carbon steel piping under internal design pressure is calculated per ASME B31.3 Section 304.1.2:
t_{min} = rac{P \cdot D}{2(S \cdot E + P \cdot Y)} + c
where P is internal design gauge pressure, D is outside diameter, S is allowable stress value from ASME Section II Part D, E is longitudinal weld joint quality factor, Y is material coefficient (0.4 for ferritic steels below 482°C), and c is corrosion allowance. If measured wall thickness {actual} \le t_{min}$, immediate derating or replacement is mandatory.

## 2. Corrosion Under Insulation (CUI) Inspection

### 2.1 High-Risk Temperature Ranges and Visual Indicators
Insulated carbon steel and low-alloy piping operating between -4°C and 175°C (25°F to 350°F) are classified as high risk for Corrosion Under Insulation (CUI). Visual screening must look for insulation jacket damage, missing sealant, water staining, and biological growth. Pulsed Eddy Current (PEC) and Profile Radiography (PRT) shall be used for non-invasive screening without removing cladding.

### 2.2 Remediation and Coating Application
When insulation is stripped and pitting corrosion is detected, the surface must be abrasive blasted to SSPC-SP 10 (Near-White Metal) and coated with a two-coat epoxy phenolic or thermal spray aluminum (TSA) system before re-insulating with non-wicking cellular glass insulation.

## 3. Storage Tank Floor and Shell Inspection (API 653)

### 3.1 Magnetic Flux Leakage (MFL) Floor Scanning
Atmospheric storage tank bottom plates must be scanned 100% using Magnetic Flux Leakage (MFL) floor crawlers. Plate underside pitting deeper than 50% of nominal thickness requires patch plate welding or bottom plate replacement according to API 653 Section 9.
